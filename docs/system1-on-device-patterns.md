# System 1 on device: patterns (skeleton)

Status: **skeleton** (updated 14/11/2026 with the study-003 confirmatory and the decided 001 F3). Every pattern
below carries one or more of these tags:

- **measured (confirmatory, test)**: a pre-registered hypothesis decided once on a held-out set under a fixed rule
  (Holm, one-sided alpha 0.025). Today this covers study 001 F3 and study 003.
- **measured (study 001, dev)**: a number from lab-s12's development split, exploratory, one to three models,
  with its source file. Not a confirmed result.
- **descriptive (test)**: a number read on the held-out test outside any hypothesis family (no decision rule).
- **projection**: computed from dev data under a stated assumption, not measured.
- **pending study 002** / **pending study 004**: the hypothesis that will measure it. Study 002 status: only the five
  T1 specialist units of stage A were launched (the int4 dev-B step was fixed after a KV-shape crash); nothing of 002 is decided, and T2 (H5-H7, technique bench) has not been launched. Study 004
  (S2 evidence under int4 in the browser) is running; only one dev cell exists.
- **not measured**: no number exists; the pattern is a mechanism, not a recommendation.

No pattern is a recommendation until a confirmatory claim exists. Sources are lab-s12 study reports (not published
yet) unless stated otherwise; the library mechanism for
each pattern is named where one exists. Laws (GDPR, LGPD, CCPA) are always reported side by side, never pooled or
compared.

Terms. **System 1 (S1)**: a typed decision (predicate, choice, score) read in one forward pass, from the logits of
the label tokens or from a pointer head over the hidden state. **System 2 (S2)**: text generated by the same weights.
**Margin**: study 002 defines it as p(1st label) − p(2nd label) (prereg 002, T1); the dev gate probe ranked by the raw
score gap instead (it does not saturate). The library exposes both (`margin`, `rawMargin`).

---

## 1. Expose the margin as a field

Library: `answer.margin`, `answer.rawMargin`.

- **measured (study 001, dev)**: where the readout works, the int4 margin predicts int4-vs-bf16 decision flips with
  AUC 0.86–0.99 (SmolLM3-3B adapted heads, S5-nano joint head); where the model is at chance (SmolLM2-135M adapted;
  EuroLLM-1.7B base on GDPR/CCPA) AUC is 0.48–0.72. Ranking by raw score gap; WebGPU, onnxruntime-web 1.31.1 (one
  SmolLM3 base cell on 1.31.0-dev). Source: the lab-s12 gate probe report §1.
- **pending study 002**: H2 (family G2, AUC > 0.70) on fresh tasks, with the probability-gap margin.

## 2. Escalate the lowest-margin share from int4 to a more precise graph

Library: `escalate: { below, to: { build } }`, `thresholdFromQuantile`.

- **measured (study 001, dev)**: against **bf16** (no int8 graph was run), escalating the lowest-margin 15% catches
  57–100% of int4 flips where the head works, against 15% for a random gate (S5-nano joint: 100% on all three
  laws). But in those models flips are 3–6% of items and in about half of them the int4 answer is the right one:
  at most ~1 pp of accuracy is at stake. Source: the lab-s12 gate probe report §1 and verdict.
- **pending study 002**: H4 / G4a: int4 -> int8 (weights only, models ≤ 1.7 B), thresholds fixed as the 10% and 15%
  quantiles of dev-B int4 margins (15% is the test), against a seeded random gate; bytes resident and median/p95
  latency per item on the Mac, with and without escalation.
- No default threshold exists. A threshold is per model, build, readout and task.

## 3. Escalate to a larger model by margin (cascade)

Library: `escalate: { to: { build } }` covers builds of one manifest; a different model is a separate `S12`
passed as `escalate.target`.

- **measured (study 001, dev, exploratory)**: small model decides, lowest-margin items go to a cloud comparator
  (Claude): +2 to +4 pp of balanced accuracy over escalating the same share at random, at 15–30% escalation, with
  intervals excluding 0 in several cells; the comparator was not stronger than the good small models on GDPR/LGPD,
  and the best cells are on LGPD (10 clusters, optimistic intervals). Rates were chosen after seeing the curves.
  Source: the lab-s12 gate probe report §2.
- Not in study 002. A cloud comparator also takes the data off the device.

## 4. Readout choice (head vs logits) and int4 stability: no default

- **measured (study 001, dev), mixed**: SmolLM3-3B adapted (head) flips 3.7–6.4% int4 vs bf16, the base (logits)
  12.0–16.4%; but these are different checkpoints, so adaptation and readout are confounded. On the
  same weights (CT0), the head flips **more**: logits 5.8–13.4%, head 12.2–19.8% (one model, synthetic data; prereg
  002, "What we already know").
- **measured (confirmatory, test), 001 F3**: int4 WebGPU vs bf16 reference, Holm m = 36, margin −5 pp: S1 accuracy
  (head) non-inferior on all three laws for SmolLM3-3B, S5-nano and Jurema-7B; head decision flips 2.5–5.1% of items
  in those three models (descriptive). Source: the study 001 F3 test report. The Δ mixes quantization,
  runtime and hardware (bf16 = PyTorch on an A40, int4 = onnxruntime-web on an M2 Pro); it is not a pure int4 effect.
- **pending study 002**: descriptive only (the main readout in T1 is the head; logits only in H3).

## 5. Do not promise "the decision is more robust than the written answer"

- **measured (study 001, dev)**: SmolLM3-3B joint, GDPR, int4 vs bf16: on the 491 items extractable on both sides,
  the answer extracted from generated text flips in 3.3% and the S1 decision in 3.7%: no advantage. What
  quantization breaks in the written answer is the **format** (17 of 33 flips were int4 no longer answering
  Yes/No). Source: prereg 002, "What we already know".
- **descriptive (test), study 001, on the strict citation metric M3**: the 001 "valid citation" metric M3 is a
  strict-format check, not a test of whether the model can point to evidence: the lab's citation checker requires the
  **whole** E2 reply to be a literal substring of the passage, ≥ 5 words, at most one sentence and ≤ 50% of the unit
  (`paper/REVIEW-swarm-001.md`, R2-2). On the test it is 0.0–14.3% for the joint s1 models, against 42.3% (GDPR) for
  "quote the first sentence" (the study 001 test report, "M3" table). A low M3 does not show that a model cannot
  cite; study 003 measures the span the reply quotes instead (pattern 14).
- **pending study 002**: H1a / H1b.

## 6. Typed decisions instead of a whole generated JSON, for small models

- **measured (outside the lab, small n)**: SmolLM3-3B q4f16 in the browser, S1 decisions + short S2 vs the same
  model producing a whole JSON: 10/33 vs 0/33 tasks fully right (exact McNemar p ≈ 0.002); prompt effort was
  asymmetric. Gemma-2-2B: whole JSON (MediaPipe, int8) 42/100 vs the S1 mechanism (int4 WebGPU) 45/100, not
  separated (p = 0.75); runtime and quantization also differ between those arms. Source:
  `poc-webaiagent/results/SUMMARY-v2.md`, `poc-webaiagent/README.md`.
- **pending study 002**: H3 (G3) with one frozen prompt protocol for every arm.

## 7. Reuse the shared prefix, but validate it per backend

Library: shared prefix across questions (`snapshot` + `prefillFrom`), `s.generate()` from the kept prefill.

- **measured (study 002 smoke run, not a result)**: ORT CPU, int4 graph, NDA prefix cached: decisions 100% equal,
  |Δ margin| = 0, 8.3–9.1x faster (Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B, SmolLM3-3B). PyTorch bf16 on GPU: **not** validated
  (95.9–98.8% agreement, max |Δ margin| 0.12–0.58). Source:.
- Not measured: WebGPU, genai in the browser, LiteRT.
- **pending study 002**: the check on the frozen specialist (amendment v1.2, item 15).

## 8. Check CPU vs WebGPU on the same graph

- **measured (study 001, dev)**: SmolLM3-3B joint, same int4 graph, head decisions equal in 506/508, 594/604 and
  419/420 items (98.3–99.8%), on an ARM Mac. Source:.
- **pending study 002**: agreement per cell for the finalists; < 98% in a cell is investigated before use.

## 9. Latency budget for S1

- **measured (study 001, dev)**: Chrome WebGPU on a Mac, cold load per run, median per item with the whole prompt
  prefilled: SmolLM3-3B 361–466 ms, SmolLM2-135M 29–48 ms. CPU numbers from the same session are not citable (CPU
  under contention). Source:.
- **pending study 002**: median and p95 with and without escalation, on the Mac.

## 10. Set the decision threshold for the deployment prevalence

- **projection** (dev reweighted, not measured): on balanced dev sets the heads are reasonably calibrated on
  GDPR/LGPD (ECE 0.02–0.08) and overconfident on CCPA (0.09–0.18). Keeping p > 0.5 at ~4% positives, projected
  precision falls to 0.15–0.43; a prior-corrected threshold (p > 0.96) or a dev-fitted one helps, but the projection
  assumes each class looks the same at natural prevalence. Source: the lab-s12 gate probe report §3.
- Not in study 002 as a hypothesis; needs a sample at natural prevalence.

## 11. Test whether the model already separates the labels (zero-shot by logits) before adapting it

- **measured (study 001, dev), hypothesis only**: with LoRA + head for one epoch, models learned what the base
  already separated by logits and did not learn what it did not (n = 7 models, cut-off seen after the data); two
  of those learned with two epochs. Source: prereg 002, "What we already know". Evidence against the general
  claim: arXiv 2402.02314.
- **pending study 002**: H5.

## 13. Escalate from S1 to S2 by margin (generate only when the margin is low)

Library: `escalate: { to: "generate" }` (continues from the kept decision prefill where the backend keeps it;
the text is attached, the typed decision is not changed).

- **not measured**, and **not part of study 002**. The library provides the mechanism; whether the generated text
  is better than the S1 decision on low-margin items is unknown.
- What is known about the generated evidence itself is in pattern 14 (bf16) and pending in 004 (int4).

## 14. Score S2 evidence against a content-blind rule, not only against chance

Applies to any S2 step that quotes evidence from the input (the E2 "quote the passage" reply).

- **measured (confirmatory, test), study 003**: on pool N (positives of 001's test split never taken to E2; GDPR
  350 eligible items in 28 policies, LGPD 806 in 15 companies), the span quoted in E2 is extracted with the frozen
  extractor (XA = longest literal word run of E2 found in the passage) and scored for "concise hit" of the
  human-annotated span. Holm m = 12, one-sided alpha 0.025, wild cluster bootstrap-t.
  - Against **chance** (an arbitrary sentence; H3a-chance): confirmed in 5 of 6 model × law cells. GDPR: SmolLM3-3B
    +18.7 pp, S5-nano +23.0, Jurema-7B +20.7. LGPD: S5-nano +21.2, Jurema-7B +18.6; SmolLM3-3B +8.9 inconclusive
    (p 0.0042 above its Holm step).
  - Against a **content-blind rule** (H3a-heuristic; GDPR "largest sentence that fits 50% of the passage", LGPD
    "middle segment"): inconclusive in 6 of 6 (GDPR +6.3, +10.6, +8.3 pp; LGPD −3.7, +8.6, +6.0; every lower bound
    below 0). None inferior.
  - Concise hit, GDPR: 44.9 / 49.1 / 46.9% vs chance 26.1% and the rule 38.6%. LGPD: 40.7 / 53.0 / 50.4% vs chance
    31.8% and the rule 44.4%. Source: the study 003 confirmatory report.
  - **Frozen reading**: no model × law supports "the model points to the evidence"; where chance is beaten, only
    "above the chance of an arbitrary sentence, without showing it reads the requirement". The heuristic test was
    expected to be underpowered (prereg 003); inconclusive is not "equal to the rule".
- **Practical consequence for the library**: a reply that quotes text is better than chance at landing on the
  annotated span, but it has not been shown to beat a rule that ignores the question. Do not present the quoted span
  as an explanation of the S1 decision.
- **measured (descriptive, 003(b))**: "which LGPD article" (8 requirements, gold checked against the Planalto text):
  article level 0/8 to 7/8 depending on model and variant, but a constant answer "18" scores 8/8, so this is not
  evidence of legal knowledge. GDPR not evaluable (official EUR-Lex text not frozen). Source: same report.
- **pending study 004**: whether the int4 browser build keeps quoting a literal span (H4a, non-inferiority, margin
  −5 pp, Holm m = 6). Only one dev cell exists (SmolLM3-3B GDPR: extractable 98.0% in both; concise hit 48.0% → 40.9%,
  n = 127, no power), and its Δ mixes precision, runtime and batching (bf16 batched PyTorch vs int4 item-by-item
  WebGPU). Source: the study 004 dev report (dev, descriptive).
